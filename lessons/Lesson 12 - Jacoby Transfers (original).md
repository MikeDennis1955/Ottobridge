Lesson 12: Jacoby Transfers

Motivation: No-trump openings (note: we include openings of 2NT as well as sequences like 2C - P - 2D - P - 2NT showing a balanced hand a bit stronger than a basic 2NT opening, say 23-24 HCP) provide responder with a great deal of information. With the narrow opening range and the promise of a balanced hand pattern, responder is well-equipped to visualize a narrow range of possible final contracts. We have defined the Stayman convention as one tool to sort out the question of whether to play in NT or a major suit (minor-suit contracts are less attractive because of the necessity of taking 11 tricks to make a game). 

But a disadvantage of this method is that it will sometimes leave the contract to be played by responder. This is disadvantageous in a couple of ways. The NT opener will usually be significantly stronger than responder’s hand, and it is strategically better to conceal those high card assets from the defense than to expose them in the dummy. At a tactical level, the balanced hand with significant and distributed high cards will often gain a trick from having the opening lead come up to it (as declarer) rather than through it (as dummy). We need an approach to no-trump auctions that will maximize the likelihood that the NT opener will win the declaration. Thus:

Jacoby Transfers  When your partner opens 1NT (mutatis mutandis for 2NT), with a 5-card+ major suit, respond in the suit below your major, i.e., respond 2D with a heart suit and respond 2H with a spade suit. This shows 5+ cards in the target suit and requires the NT opener to bid 2 of the target suit. Notice that when responder uses this bid, opener will end up playing the contract whether in the major suit or in NT. Responder needs to bear in mind that the opener who accepts the transfer is not promising more than the original two pieces in that suit than guaranteed by the opening NT bid. Opener is simply doing what they’re told. In a competitive game, opener is required to notify the opponents when responder uses this bid by saying “Transfer” when the bid is made.

That’s all there is to this convention, but the implications are important. Let’s see some examples of this in action. Note that Jacoby transfers are marked with an asterisk.

1.  1NT - P  - 2D* - P

 2H - P - P - P
This is how responder handles a weak hand with a long major suit. Recall that previously, we used a direct 2H (or 2S) bid to play such hands, with opener expected to pass. Same result here, except that the NT hand remains concealed. Note that with transfers, we’ve given up the option of using 2D to describe a weak hand with long diamonds, just as we’ve done by assigning a conventional meaning to 2C Stayman.

2. 1NT - P - 2C 

Stayman is still in effect, except that (most of the time) responder will have one-or both 4-card majors, i.e., not 5 cards or longer, since then they’ll generally prefer the transfer route.

3. 1NT - P-  2H* - P -

2S - P -  3S -
On this hand, responder is showing a 6-card spade suit with invitational values (8-9 pts). With more than that, responder would just bid the game directly. With fewer, she would just pass 2S, reasonably confident that no game is in sight. She knows that her side as at least 8 spades, so this is probably safer than NT. Opener will pass 3S with a minimum and bid on to 4S with a maximum.

4. 1NT - P - 2D* - P -

2H - P - 3NT - P -
P
What can we tell from this auction?  Responder has exactly 5 hearts and at least 10 points. We know this because a) with more than 5 hearts, responder would have “raised” the suit b) with fewer than 10 points, responder would’ve passed or made an invitational bid rather than committing directly to game. Also note that 3NT is just a suggestion; responder is asking opener to choose between a game in hearts or NT. With only two hearts, opener will accept 3NT. With 3 hearts (and definitely with 4) opener will generally prefer the suit contract.

5. 1NT - P - 2H* - P

         2S - P - 2NT
         Responder shows a hand with exactly 5 spades and 8 - 9 HCP.
Opener has two distinct choices to make at this point. He can accept the invitation by bidding 3NT (with a maximum) or passing 2NT (with a minimum) if he has just two spades. With 3 or more spades, opener can decline the game invitation by bidding 3S or accept the invitation by bidding 4S. Note that the 3S alternative is not suggestive of interest in game. Responder has shown 5 spades and 8-9 points. Opener has simply determined that a spade contract is preferable to NT and that the combined points fall short of game values.

6. 2NT - P - 3H* - P -

3S - P- 4S
For completeness, we include an auction starting with 2NT. Note that because the level is higher, we have less room to fine-tune the invitational-vs-game-forcing hands for responder. However the distributional aspects are the same. Here, responder is showing a 6-card spade suit and something like 4-8 points, enough for a good shot at game opposite a 2NT opener.

7. 1NT - P - 2H* - P -

2S - P - 3D
On this auction, responder promises enough for game, and maybe more. The new suit is natural and game-forcing, and might be a prelude to an exploration of slam possibilities. Opener should rebid 3S with decent support in spades (3 or more) .
